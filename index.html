The woman I love

About my Hija

Every little detail I never want to forget.

Hazel Lyn G. Angelo

Public Elementary Teacher · Palayan City

She wakes up early, packs her patience and her lessons, and heads to Marcos Village Elementary School in Palayan City, Nueva Ecija — where she spends her days teaching young minds how to read, count, and believe in themselves.

She is patient in a way that amazes me, kind in a way that changes rooms, and strong in a way she never gives herself credit for. She gives so much of herself to her students every day — and still comes home with enough love left over for me.

She likes to travel and to eat — new places, new food, new stories. Her favorite drink is matcha. She does not drink coffee. Ever. (I've stopped asking.)

And she loves my cooking. She loves how I serve her — the plate set down in front of her, the small "eat na, Hija" that comes with it. I make her breakfast and lunch, and honestly, watching her eat is one of my favorite parts of the day.

We are building our future together — one meal, one plan, one day at a time. And her dream destination is Korea. One day, Hija. I promise.

I call her Hija. She calls me Hjio. Two silly little names that mean more to me than any word ever written.

The first letter · Always open

To my Hija,

I don't know if you realize how much you changed my life. Before you, my days were just days. Now they have a shape — they start with me making your breakfast, they have a middle where I wonder how your class is going, and they end with me hoping you ate properly and that your feet aren't too tired.

I've watched you get up early, tired, and still show up for your students at Marcos Village Elementary School. I've seen you explain the same thing three times with the same patience as the first. I've seen you come home drained and still ask if I've eaten. That's who you are, Hazel. You give, and you give, and you give.

And somehow you still let me take care of you. You let me cook. You let me serve you. You say thank you like I did something huge, when really I just wanted to make sure you're fed. That's the part that gets me — you love the small things, and the small things are all I have to give.

So this little corner of the internet exists for one reason: to remind you, on the hard days, that someone notices. Someone is proud of you. Someone thinks you're the best thing that ever happened to September 5, 2021.

We're building something, you and me. Slowly. Carefully. With breakfast and lunch and long talks and a trip to Korea at the end of the road.

Thank you for being my Hija. I'll keep being your Hjio for as long as you'll let me.

Mahal kita, palagi. — Hjio 💚
